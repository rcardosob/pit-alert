# PIT ALERT — Landing Page Creative Brief

## 1. Objective

Design and build a highly visual landing page whose primary objective is to convince intraday traders to download PIT ALERT.

This should NOT feel like a conventional SaaS landing page.

Avoid the standard structure of:

Hero → Features → Benefits → Testimonials → Pricing → FAQ.

The experience should feel closer to an interactive editorial campaign for a premium motorsport or technology brand.

The landing page should communicate the product primarily through visual storytelling, movement, typography, atmosphere, and a small amount of carefully selected copy.

---

# 2. Product

PIT ALERT is a desktop application for intraday traders.

During trading sessions, important economic events such as CPI, FOMC decisions, employment reports, inflation data, and other high-impact releases can generate extreme market volatility within seconds.

The problem is simple:

A trader focused on the chart can easily lose track of the economic calendar.

PIT ALERT monitors scheduled economic events and warns the trader before an important event occurs.

An alert contains:

* Event identification
* Impact level
* Audible warning
* Countdown to the event

Users can configure:

* Currencies
* Impact levels
* Alert anticipation

PIT ALERT remains active while the trader operates.

The product does NOT:

* Predict market direction
* Generate BUY signals
* Generate SELL signals
* Recommend trades
* Tell the trader what to do

Its job is much simpler:

**Warn the trader that conditions may be about to change.**

The trader decides what to do.

---

# 3. Core Brand Idea

## LEAVE THE TRACK

The conceptual universe of PIT ALERT is motorsport.

Trading is the race.

The trader is the driver.

The market is the track.

PIT ALERT is the pit wall.

This distinction is fundamental.

PIT ALERT does not drive the car.

PIT ALERT does not decide where the driver should go.

PIT ALERT watches information outside the driver's immediate field of attention and warns when something important is approaching.

Entering the pits does not mean abandoning the race.

It is a tactical decision.

The core philosophy is:

> Sometimes the best trade is leaving the track.

---

# 4. The Role of PIT ALERT

The trader's attention belongs on:

* Price
* Market structure
* Execution
* Risk
* The trade

PIT ALERT watches external events.

Conceptually:

TRADER
→ watches the market

PIT ALERT
→ watches the track

When an important event approaches, PIT ALERT does not say:

BUY

SELL

BULLISH

BEARISH

Instead, it simply communicates:

> PIT.

The trader makes the decision.

# 5. Primary Storytelling

The landing page should behave as a sequence of cinematic scenes rather than a collection of website sections.

Scrolling should progressively reveal the story.

The experience should communicate PIT ALERT before explaining PIT ALERT.

---

# SCENE 01 — THE TRACK

The opening should be extremely minimal.

Large visual composition.

Possible visual language:

* Asphalt
* Pit lane
* Track markings
* Motion
* Racing lights
* Abstract track geometry

Avoid immediately showing a full Formula 1 car.

The motorsport metaphor should initially be atmospheric rather than literal.

Primary copy:

> Sometimes the best trade
> is leaving the track.

Small brand presence:

PIT ALERT

The visitor should immediately feel:

speed, focus, risk, precision.

---

# SCENE 02 — THE DRIVER

Introduce the trader without necessarily showing a person sitting in front of monitors.

Represent concentration through:

* Price movement
* Charts
* Market numbers
* Motion
* Execution
* Visual velocity

Primary message:

> Your eyes belong on the market.

Then reveal:

> Not on the economic calendar.

This establishes the problem.

The trader cannot continuously monitor every external source while concentrating on execution.

---

# SCENE 03 — SOMETHING IS COMING

The atmosphere changes.

An external event appears.

Example:

HIGH IMPACT

US CPI

03:00

The countdown begins.

03:00
02:59
02:58
02:57

The countdown should become a major visual element.

Small copy:

> PIT ALERT is watching.

This is the moment where the visitor should begin understanding the product without requiring a feature explanation.

PIT ALERT is watching something the trader should not have to continuously watch.

---

# SCENE 04 — LEAVE THE TRACK

This is the visual climax of the landing page.

The motorsport metaphor becomes explicit.

The visual composition suggests leaving the racing line and entering the pit lane.

This can be communicated through:

* Track geometry
* Movement
* Pit lane markings
* Camera movement
* Motion graphics
* Scroll-driven animation

The dominant message:

> PIT.

Then:

> No predictions.
> No signals.
> No trading advice.

Followed by:

> Just a warning when the track is about to change.

This scene defines both what PIT ALERT is and what PIT ALERT refuses to be.

---

# SCENE 05 — THE PRODUCT

Only after the concept has been established should we clearly show the actual application.

Use a large product screenshot or application representation.

Do not surround it with excessive UI decoration.

Highlight only the essential capabilities:

Economic events

Custom alerts

Impact filters

Countdown

The product should now feel like the technological implementation of the story the visitor has just experienced.

---

# SCENE 06 — THE FUTURE

PIT ALERT currently monitors scheduled economic events.

However, the product vision extends beyond scheduled events.

Future PIT alerts may originate from:

* Breaking news
* Relevant publications
* Unexpected market events
* External signals capable of producing sudden volatility

The concept:

> Today, scheduled events.
> Tomorrow, anything that can move the market.

This section should be subtle.

Do not make unreleased functionality appear to be currently available.

It represents product direction.

---

# SCENE 07 — BACK TO RACING

Return to visual calm.

The race continues.

PIT ALERT remains in the background watching.

Possible closing message:

> Stay focused on the race.
> We'll watch the track.

Alternative:

> Know when to pit.

Then:

PIT ALERT

Primary CTA:

> Download PIT ALERT

Secondary information:

> Windows

The download CTA should be visually dominant but elegant.

# 6. Visual Direction

The landing page should feel:

* Minimal
* Cinematic
* Premium
* Technical
* Precise
* Fast
* Controlled
* Sophisticated

The visual reference is NOT a typical trading website.

Avoid:

* Green/red trading clichés
* Crypto aesthetics
* Lamborghini imagery
* Neon overload
* Dense dashboards
* Generic fintech gradients
* Excessive glassmorphism
* Stock photography of traders
* Full-screen candlestick charts everywhere
* Generic AI aesthetics

The desired feeling is closer to:

Premium motorsport editorial campaign
×
Financial technology
×
Precision engineering

Think more in terms of a premium automotive campaign than a trading SaaS template.

---

# 7. Visual Language

Suggested base palette:

* Carbon black
* Asphalt gray
* Off-white
* Neutral metallic tones

Use ONE strong alert/accent color.

The alert color should have functional meaning.

It should represent:

attention
risk
PIT ALERT activation

Do not decorate the entire interface with the accent color.

Reserve it for moments that matter.

---

# 8. Typography

Typography should play a major visual role.

Use:

* Large headlines
* Strong scale contrast
* Minimal copy
* Generous negative space
* Editorial composition

Some scenes may contain only a few words.

That is intentional.

Do not attempt to fill empty space.

Empty space is part of the visual language.

---

# 9. Motorsport Imagery

Do not turn the website into a Formula 1 fan page.

Motorsport is a metaphor.

Prefer fragments:

* Pit lane lines
* Asphalt texture
* Racing lights
* Track geometry
* Tire details
* Motion blur
* Pit wall environments
* Empty track
* Overhead track views

A complete race car should be used sparingly, if at all.

The visual system should still make sense even if recognizable racing cars are removed.

---

# 10. Motion

Motion is an important storytelling mechanism.

Potential techniques:

* Scroll-driven transitions
* Countdown animation
* Track lines moving through the viewport
* Subtle parallax
* Elements entering and leaving the racing line
* Speed changes
* Controlled motion blur
* Typography reacting to scroll
* Transition from market visualization to pit lane

Motion should communicate:

speed → warning → decision → calm.

Avoid animation merely for decoration.

Every significant animation should contribute to the story.

---

# 11. Emotional Rhythm

The experience should follow this sequence:

CALM

↓

FOCUS

↓

SOMETHING IS COMING

↓

TENSION

↓

PIT ALERT

↓

DECISION

↓

CALM

↓

BACK TO RACING

The page should therefore have rhythm.

Not every section should be visually intense.

Silence and empty space are necessary to make the alert moment powerful.

---

# 12. Product Positioning

PIT ALERT should NOT be positioned as another trading indicator.

It should occupy its own category:

**situational awareness for traders.**

Its value proposition is not:

"Make better predictions."

It is:

**"Know when market conditions may be about to change."**

The product protects attention.

---

# 13. Core Copy Territory

These phrases establish the desired language.

They are creative direction, not necessarily all mandatory final copy.

> Sometimes the best trade is leaving the track.

> Your eyes belong on the market.

> PIT ALERT is watching.

> PIT.

> No predictions. No signals. No trading advice.

> Just a warning when the track is about to change.

> Stay focused on the race. We'll watch the track.

> Know when to pit.

The copy should remain short.

Avoid paragraphs explaining functionality unless absolutely necessary.

The visual experience should carry much of the explanation.

---

# 14. Important Conceptual Constraint

Never portray PIT ALERT as knowing what the market will do after an event.

PIT ALERT detects or anticipates an EVENT.

It does not predict the MARKET REACTION.

This distinction is fundamental to the credibility of the product.

Correct:

"An important event is approaching."

Incorrect:

"Volatility is coming."

Correct:

"Conditions may change."

Incorrect:

"The market is about to fall."

---

# 15. Conversion Objective

Despite the conceptual storytelling, this remains a product landing page.

The final objective is:

**DOWNLOAD PIT ALERT**

The visitor should finish the experience understanding three things:

1. Important events can occur while I am focused on trading.

2. PIT ALERT watches those events for me.

3. PIT ALERT warns me so I can decide whether to stay in the market or enter the pits.

If those three ideas are understood, the landing page has succeeded.

---

# 16. Design Principle

When choosing between:

MORE INFORMATION

and

STRONGER COMMUNICATION

prefer stronger communication.

When choosing between:

MORE UI

and

MORE ATMOSPHERE

prefer atmosphere.

When choosing between:

EXPLAINING THE METAPHOR

and

MAKING THE USER FEEL THE METAPHOR

make the user feel it.

The goal is not to build a website about PIT ALERT.

The goal is to make the visitor experience the moment when a PIT ALERT matters.
